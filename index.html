📊 Executive Summary

This comprehensive analysis examines 237 pharmaceutical formulations from the Al-Aqrabadhin, the world's first systematic pharmaceutical formulary created in the 9th-11th century CE during the Islamic Golden Age. This groundbreaking text established pharmacy as a distinct profession and introduced principles still used today.

46 formulas with Very High Research Priority represent untapped opportunities for modern pharmaceutical research.

  • 1. Polyherbal Synergy VERY HIGH IMPACT

    Feasibility: High | Funding: Medium

  • 2. Dose-Finding Studies HIGH IMPACT

    Research Gap: Historical doses (dirham, mithqal) not converted to modern standardized doses

    Key Question: What is optimal saffron dose for depression? (Ancient: 0.5-2 dirham)

    Feasibility: High | Funding: Medium-High

  • 3. Route Optimization MEDIUM-HIGH IMPACT

    Research Gap: Multiple routes (nasal, topical, oral) not systematically compared

    Key Question: Does nasal administration provide faster CNS effects? (Modern research supports this)

    Feasibility: Medium | Funding: Low-Medium

  • 4. Quality Standardization VERY HIGH IMPACT

    Research Gap: No modern quality standards for ancient recipes

    Key Question: Can WHO develop pharmacopeial standards for validated ancient formulas?

    Feasibility: HIGH | Funding: High (WHO/Regulatory interest)

    Research Gap: Modern drugs + ancient herbs = unknown interactions

    Key Question: Do ancient herbs interact with modern cardiovascular drugs?

    Feasibility: HIGH | Funding: Very High (FDA concern)

  • 6. Personalized Medicine Genomics VERY HIGH IMPACT

    Key Question: Does ancient "hot/cold constitution" correlate with genetic polymorphisms?

    Feasibility: HIGH | Funding: Very High (Pharmacogenomics)

  • 7. Network Pharmacology VERY HIGH IMPACT

    Research Gap: Multi-target effects of polyherbal formulas not studied

    Key Question: Do ancient formulas address diseases through network effects vs. single targets?

    Feasibility: HIGH (Computational methods available) | Funding: High (NIH)

    Feasibility: HIGH | Funding: High (FDA/EMA priority)
